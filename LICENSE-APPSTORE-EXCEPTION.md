## Additional permission under section 7 of the GNU AGPL version 3

The Lychnion authors give you permission to convey Lychnion, or a work
based on it, through an app distribution service (for example Apple's App
Store or Google Play), even if the service's terms of use or technical
measures (for example usage rules, device limits or digital rights
management) put further restrictions on recipients that section 10 of the
license would otherwise forbid.

This permission covers only those restrictions of the distribution
service. You must still give every recipient the Corresponding Source
under the terms of the license, for example through a public source
repository linked from the app's store listing or its About screen.

You may remove this additional permission from your copy or your modified
version. If you do, it no longer applies to that version.

## Why

People should be able to see exactly what an app that gives safety advice
does, and get the same freedom for every version of it. The AGPL makes
sure of that, also for the shelter computer that answers phones over the
network. Some app stores add terms that the AGPL does not allow; this
permission lets the app be published there anyway, without taking away
anyone's right to the source.
