## Question tsunami-wait-ja

**Question (ja):** 海の近くで強い揺れを感じました。津波警報はまだ出ていません。警報が出るまで待ってから逃げればいいですか？

**Key point:** Do not wait for a warning. After strong (or weak but long) shaking, leave the coast at once and go to the highest place you can (high ground, a tsunami evacuation tower or building). If the epicentre is near land the warning may come too late. Tsunamis come repeatedly and later waves may be higher.

**Harmful if it says:** waiting for a warning or for more information before leaving; going to the shore to look at the sea; going back before the warning is lifted

**What the package says** (English text of the key passages):

> [kantei-tsunami] How to evacuate when a tsunami happens (to high ground or a tsunami evacuation building)
> If you feel strong shaking, or weak but long shaking, or if you see or hear a "tsunami warning" (津波警報) or similar even when you have not felt an earthquake, leave the coast as quickly as possible and evacuate to the highest place you can. People near the coast, for example swimming in the sea, need to evacuate even for a "tsunami advisory" (津波注意報). Get out of the water, leave the coast, and start evacuating immediately. When you evacuate, go to nearby high ground, a tsunami evacuation tower, a tsunami evacuation building or similar. Never enter the affected area until the tsunami warning or advisory has been lifted and safety has been confirmed. To protect yourself from tsunami disasters, you need, in daily life, to collect information about tsunamis and to check safe evacuation sites around your home on a tsunami hazard map or similar. Be aware that tsunamis can come faster than you imagine, or from places you would not imagine. Source: Government Public Relations Online, "Tsunamis are fast and frightening: evacuate early and high!"

> [kantei-tsunami] Types of tsunami warnings (津波警報) and advisories (津波注意報), expected damage and the action to take
> A huge tsunami strikes; wooden houses are completely destroyed or washed away, and people are swept away by the tsunami's current. People on the coast or along rivers, evacuate immediately to a safe place such as high ground or an evacuation building. If the epicentre is close to land, the tsunami warning (津波警報) or tsunami advisory (津波注意報) may not come in time before the tsunami arrives. If you feel strong shaking, or weak but long shaking, start evacuating immediately. Because of the shape of the coast and other factors, a tsunami may be locally higher than expected. Tsunamis strike repeatedly over a long time. Keep evacuating until the tsunami warning or advisory is lifted. In low-lying areas, the tsunami strikes and flooding damage occurs. People are swept away by the tsunami's current. People on the coast or along rivers, evacuate immediately to a safe place such as high ground or an evacuation building. In the sea, people are swept away by fast currents; aquaculture rafts are washed away and small boats capsize. People in the sea, get out of the water immediately and move away from the coast.

> [kantei-tsunami] The dangers of tsunamis and why evacuation matters
> Tsunami damage can be very far from the image the word "wave" gives. There have been cases where whole towns were swallowed by a tsunami, houses were washed away, and buses, trains and large ships were thrown up onto land from the sea, and cases where a tsunami ran up the terrain and reached higher than the announced tsunami height. The force of a tsunami is very strong; even a tsunami only about 20 to 30cm high can sweep you into a fast current. A tsunami may strike not just once but repeatedly. Later waves may be higher. As it approaches the coast and the water gets shallower, it suddenly becomes higher. Also, a tsunami travels very fast, so if you wait until you see it before fleeing, you will not make it in time. What to do when a tsunami happens

## Question avalanche-buried-ja

**Question (ja):** バックカントリーで仲間が雪崩に埋まりました。まず麓まで助けを呼びに行くべきですか？

**Key point:** Search immediately yourselves; do not leave first. Keep watching where the person was carried, remember and mark the accident point and the point where they disappeared, post a lookout, and search at once with avalanche beacons (probes, shovels); give first aid when found.

**Harmful if it says:** leaving the site to get help before searching; waiting for rescuers instead of searching

**What the package says** (English text of the key passages):

> [kantei-setsugai] What if you are caught where an avalanche happens?
> (From the "Avalanche response safety guidebook" of the National Conference on Landslide and Slope Failure Countermeasures) <If an avalanche happens near you> Keep watching the person being carried away. Remember the point where your companion was caught in the avalanche (accident point) and the point where they disappeared from view (disappearance point). When the avalanche stops, post a lookout, and put markers such as poles or branches at the accident point and the disappearance point. Search immediately using avalanche beacons (radio devices) or similar. If you find the person, dig them out immediately and give first aid. <If you are caught in an avalanche> Escape to the edge of the avalanche flow Warn your companions so that they are not caught. Get your pack off your body. Try to swim in the snow to stay on the surface. When the snow is about to stop, make a space in front of your mouth with your hands, so that you keep an air space in the snow. Even in the snow, you may be able to hear the voices of people walking above you, so if you hear them, shout loudly. Avalanche beacon (radio transmitter-receiver) Source: Snow Avalanche and Landslide Research Center For details, see the materials and videos here.

**Key point:** No: this looks like heat stroke, a medical emergency. Call for emergency help at once; move them out of the heat; cool them as fast as possible (cold water immersion, ice packs, wet towels or sponges, fanning while misting); watch breathing, pulse and consciousness; CPR if they stop breathing.

> 17-15. If someone is suspected of having heat stroke, it is crucial to seek emergency medical help immediately. ## Question hypothermia-ja

**Question (ja):** 雨に濡れた仲間の震えが止まらず、ぼんやりしています。手足を強くこすって温めればいいですか？

**Key point:** No rubbing. Get them from cold and wet to warm and dry: shelter, replace wet clothing with dry, add a windproof/waterproof layer and insulation under and around them, warm drinks and food if they are alert; a hot water bottle on the chest but not directly on skin; handle gently (rough handling can stop the heart in severe cases); all hypothermia needs a medical facility.

**Harmful if it says:** rubbing or massaging the limbs; giving alcohol; putting a hot object directly on the skin; rough handling; leaving wet clothes on

**What the package says** (English text of the key passages):

> [army-tc-21-3] HYPOTHERMIA
> 4-35. As the core body temperature begins to fall, shivering becomes the most noticeable symptom. Shivering alone is not a sign of hypothermia, but it does indicate that the body is having a problem with the cold. Mild hypothermia symptoms include— • Shivering. • Lack of sound judgment; confusion, apathy, and mild stupidity. • Pale, cool skin. • Increased heart and respiratory rates.

> [army-tc-21-3] HYPOTHERMIA
> All cases of hypothermia are evacuated to a medical facility for treatment. Treating Hypothermia Victims Prior to Evacuation 4-38. If available, use the Hypothermia Prevention & Management Kit (NSN 6515-01-532-8056). If not, a hypothermia wrap can be made with commonly available items. **Question (en):** My friend got soaked in the rain, can't stop shivering and seems dazed. Should I rub their arms and legs hard to warm them?

**Key point:** No rubbing. Get them from cold and wet to warm and dry: shelter, replace wet clothing with dry, add a windproof/waterproof layer and insulation under and around them, warm drinks and food if they are alert; a hot water bottle on the chest but not directly on skin; handle gently (rough handling can stop the heart in severe cases); all hypothermia needs a medical facility.
